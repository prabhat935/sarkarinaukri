The Formula

Raw score = (correct x marks per question) - (wrong x marks deducted). Unattempted questions score nothing and lose nothing. With 2 marks per question and 0.5 deducted, a candidate with 70 correct, 18 wrong and 12 left blank scores 70 x 2 - 18 x 0.5 = 140 - 9 = 131 out of 200.

The calculator also shows what the wrong answers cost you, and your accuracy on the questions you attempted. If your accuracy is low, attempting fewer questions with more care often raises the score; our guide to negative marking and when to guess explains the expected-value maths behind that.

From Raw Score to Final Marks

  • Final answer key: after objections, some answers may be corrected and some questions dropped, which changes your raw score.
  • Normalisation: exams held across many shifts, such as most SSC and railway computer-based tests, adjust scores so that candidates in harder shifts are not disadvantaged. Your normalised marks can be a few marks above or below your raw score.
  • Sectional cut-offs: some exams, particularly bank exams, need a minimum in each section as well as overall. Check each section separately.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the score calculated with negative marking?

Raw score = (correct answers x marks per question) - (wrong answers x marks deducted). Unattempted questions score zero. For example, with 2 marks per question and 0.5 deducted, 70 correct and 18 wrong gives 140 - 9 = 131.

Will my final marks be the same as this raw score?

Not always. Exams held in many shifts are often normalised, so your final score can be a little higher or lower, and questions dropped or corrected in the final answer key also change the total.

Do unattempted questions carry negative marks?

No. Only wrong answers are penalised. A question you leave blank scores zero.
