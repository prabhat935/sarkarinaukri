How Your Score Is Calculated

  • Net WPM: only the words you typed exactly right count. Their characters, including the space after each word, are divided by five (the standard length of a "word") and by the minutes elapsed.
  • KDPH: all the characters in your answer, scaled to an hour. This shows your raw pace, whether or not the words were correct.
  • Word accuracy: correct words as a share of the words you typed. A misspelt, missed or extra word counts as an error, and a word you fix with backspace before moving on counts as correct.

Example: in a 10-minute test you type 400 words, 380 of them correct, with an average of 5.5 characters per word including the space. Net WPM is 380 x 5.5 / 5 / 10 = 41.8, and accuracy is 95%. That is above the 35 WPM line with a useful margin.

Getting From 25 to 40 WPM

  • Type with all ten fingers from the home row, without looking at the keys. Your speed drops for the first two weeks and then climbs past where you started.
  • Practise every day for 30 to 45 minutes. Short daily sessions beat one long session a week.
  • Take a full 10-minute test at least twice a week to build stamina, and note your net WPM each time.
  • Fix accuracy before speed. In the exam, a fast answer full of mistakes can fail where a steady one passes.
  • Aim for about 20% above the requirement, around 42 WPM, so nerves on the day do not take you below 35.

Frequently Asked Questions

What typing speed is required in SSC CHSL?

For Lower Division Clerk and Junior Secretariat Assistant posts, the typing test standard is 35 words per minute in English or 30 words per minute in Hindi, which is about 10,500 or 9,000 key depressions per hour. Data entry posts are tested in key depressions per hour instead.

What is KDPH?

Key depressions per hour: the number of keystrokes you would make in an hour at your current pace. 35 words per minute at five keystrokes per word is 175 keystrokes a minute, or 10,500 an hour.

Is the result here the same as the SSC result?

No. It is a practice measure. SSC uses its own software, passages and rules for counting errors, which are explained in each exam notice. Use this test to track your progress and aim for a margin above the required speed.
