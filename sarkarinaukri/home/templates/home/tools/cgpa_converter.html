Your institution's formula is what counts. There is no single national conversion. Recruiters generally accept the percentage worked out by the method your university or board prescribes, and may check it at document verification. Look for the formula on the back of your marksheet or in your university's notification, and if there is none, ask the college for an equivalence certificate.

The same CGPA can give results several points apart, which is why a notice that asks for 60% or 65% can depend on the formula. A CGPA of 6.9 is 61.5% under the AICTE formula but 65.55% under CBSE's, so check yours before you assume you qualify or not.

SGPA, CGPA and the Final Result

SGPA is the grade point average for a single semester, and CGPA is the average across all semesters so far. For eligibility, notices go by your final result, so use the final CGPA on your degree or consolidated marksheet. If you are in the final year and the notice allows final-year students to apply, you will usually need to show the final result by a date given in the notice.

Filling It in the Application Form

  • If the form has separate fields for CGPA and percentage, fill both exactly as on your documents and the conversion certificate.
  • Round only as your university does. Do not round 59.6% up to 60% to meet a requirement; the notice's cut-off is applied to the exact figure.
  • Keep the marksheet, degree and any conversion certificate together for document verification. Our document verification checklist lists what else to carry.
  • Check the rest of the eligibility in the notice too: our guide on reading a notification shows where each condition is given.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert CBSE Class 10 CGPA to percentage?

CBSE's indicative formula for the CGPA system is percentage = CGPA x 9.5. A CGPA of 8.4 is about 79.8%.

Which formula should I use for a job application?

The one your university or board prescribes, usually printed on the back of the marksheet or given in a university notification. Many recruitment notices say that candidates with a CGPA must give the percentage as per their institution's conversion norms, and the certificate may be checked at document verification.

What if my university has no conversion formula?

Ask the university or college for a certificate showing the equivalent percentage. Keep it with your marksheets for document verification, because a percentage you calculated yourself may not be accepted.
