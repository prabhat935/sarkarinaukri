The finder never says you are eligible for something the notice may not allow.

What the Finder Checks

  • Age on the right date. Each notice fixes the date on which age is counted, such as 1 January 2027 or 1 August 2026. The finder reads it from our write-up of the notice and calculates your age in completed years, months and days on that date. Where a notice gives no separate date, it uses the last date to apply and says so.
  • Minimum age. If you are below a post's minimum age on its cut-off date, the post is listed as not open to you.
  • Qualification level. Posts are tagged with the minimum level of education they need, from 10th pass to post graduate. A post that needs a higher level than yours is listed as not open to you. Final-year students are shown posts at their next level with a reminder to check whether the notice accepts final-year candidates.
  • Upper age limit. This depends on your category, disability, ex-servicemen status and, in state jobs, often on where you live. The finder shows each post's age rule and leaves that comparison to you; use the age eligibility calculator to work out your relaxed limit.

Things the Finder Cannot Check

Many posts need a particular subject or branch (for example a B.E. in Civil Engineering, or an ITI in a named trade), a minimum percentage, a GATE score, experience, physical standards or registration with a council. State recruitment usually reserves posts and age relaxation for that state's residents. Read the eligibility section of each post before you pay a fee. Our guide to reading a government job notification explains where each condition is written.

The list covers the open notifications we have written up from official notices; it is not every vacancy in the country. New posts are added to latest jobs as notices are published.
